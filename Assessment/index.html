A Brief Introduction to Jung's Cognitive Function Test

Throughout history, the question of 'Who am I?' has puzzled countless people. Around this question, human thought has sparked many brilliant ideas. The development of psychology has brought analysis frameworks based on cognitive functions and personality types, which help us better explore our core personality and respond to the demands from deep within ourselves.

Photo: Carl Jung

In the 1920s, psychology master Carl Jung created his own theory of psychological types. Jung was a disciple of Sigmund Freud, the founder of psychoanalysis, but later broke away and founded his own school of analytical psychology. Today, well-known concepts like extroversion and introversion, personality types, archetypes, and the collective unconscious all originated from Jung's innovations. Jung believed that people’s cognitive functions are divided into perceiving and judging. Perceiving means the entire process of becoming aware of objects, people, events, and ideas-in simple terms, it's gathering information; judging refers to making conclusions about what has been perceived-basically, decision-making.

In short, perceiving functions correspond to information gathering, while judging functions correspond to decision-making. Each function is further divided into four sub-functions, which forms the theoretical basis of our test.
