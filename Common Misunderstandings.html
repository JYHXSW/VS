Common Misunderstandings

Q1:Usually, does a person's assessed weak function score tend to be higher than their blind spot function, or the other way around?

According to Jung's theory of the "Eight Functions of Consciousness Structure," your intuition is completely correct: **in the vast majority of cases, the test score for the inferior function (4th function) is significantly higher than that of the blind spot function (7th function).** This is not only normal, but if a situation arises where the "blind spot scores higher than the inferior," it usually indicates a serious distortion in the test results.
To help you fully understand this phenomenon, we need to look at it from two dimensions:"depth of consciousness" and "mental energy investment."
1. Inferior Function (4th function): Weak, but I "acknowledge" it
- Position: It sits at the very end of the “yang four functions,” acting as the “last gate” between consciousness and the unconscious. - Psychological state: You feel a strong sense of shame and an implicit desire toward it. You know you're not good at it, but you acknowledge that "this is an issue I need to face." - Behavior in tests: When you see questions about the inferior function (for example, an INTP facing Fe items: “I’m worried that my words or actions might offend others”), you would honestly check 'often true,' because your consciousness clearly recognizes this shortfall consistently interferes with your life. - Score level: Medium-high (because it is frequently recognized consciously).
2. Blind Spot Function (7th function): Weak and completely "denied"
Position: It lies deep within the “yin four functions,” completely repressed in the unconscious as the “joker function.” - Psychological state: You have total disregard and physiological aversion toward it. You not only perform terribly but also don’t consider it important at all. When called upon to use this function by external situations, you feel extremely impatient. - Behavior in tests: When you see questions about the blind spot function (for example, an INTP facing Se items: “I enjoy responding immediately in fast-changing environments”), you instinctively check 'not important at all' or 'rarely true,'because deep down you think, “Who cares about this anyway?” - Score level: Significantly low (usually lower than all other functions).
3. Intuitive Data Illustration (with a typical INTP as an example)
If an INTP (Ti-Ne-Si-Fe) takes a high-quality eight-function test, the score curve usually forms a "W" or "U" shape:
- High score range (3 functions): Ti, Ne (core highs), Si (relatively high, as a backup).
- Mid score range (2 functions): Te (5th in the yin functions, often used as a tool), Fe (4th function, medium-low but noticeable to self).
- Lowest point (1 function): Se (7th function, absolutely the lowest, far below Fe).
You'll definitely notice: an INTP's Fe, although much lower than Ti, always firmly sits above Se.
