Perceiving Prospecting

First, the perceiving process is divided into two types: sensing, which is directly achieved through the senses, and intuition, which is indirectly realized through subconscious functions in the mind. The former is concrete, realistic, and detail-oriented, while the latter is abstract, innovative, and focuses on the big picture. According to Jung's theory, both sensing and intuition can be either extraverted, directed outward toward the world, or introverted, directed inward toward the mind. Therefore, the first four dimensions in our test include Extraverted Sensing (Se), Introverted Sensing (Si), Extraverted Intuition (Ne), and Introverted Intuition (Ni). Unlike Jung, we believe that everyone possesses all four cognitive functions at the same time, just with varying strengths and weaknesses. Our test quantitatively estimates the relative strengths of these functions to assess personality.


Extraverted Sensing (Se)

This function is mainly responsible for being sensitive to changes in the external environment and reacting to sensory stimuli. People with high Se often notice subtle changes in those around them easily; even a new ring or minor changes in body shape will catch their attention at a glance. They often excel in rhythm and manual skills, naturally keeping in sync with the surroundings, and have a keen insight into subtle, tangible aesthetics like music, scents, and food. People with high Se are often called artistic creators, as they have a unique ability and talent in enjoying and producing sensory experiences.

Introverted Sensing (Si)

This function mainly handles the recall of past details and reliance on habits, experience, and tradition. In simple terms, it's like 'memory' - although it's important to note that Si isn't directly tied to raw memory ability. Si mainly reflects the degree of attention and sensitivity to past details. People with high Si not only remember things well but also rely on these memories as important references in decision-making. They prefer stability, aren't bored with repetitive daily tasks, are loyal to traditions, have a strong sense of responsibility, and maintain existing order. Therefore, people with high Si are often called traditionalists. In a nutshell, they give off a very reliable vibe.


Extraverted Intuition (Ne)

Primarily responsible for spotting connections between things that aren't directly related. In short, it's "divergent thinking."" People with high Ne switch their thoughts frequently between different topics, are good at making associations and thinking outside the box, and can always come up with new ideas on the fly, sometimes making it hard for others to keep up with their leaps. For example, you might be discussing the latest TV show, and suddenly they jump to a joke about the China-US trade war and talk about it with great enthusiasm. If someone tends to make these kinds of jumps, it's usually a sign of high extraverted intuition. In real life, they're often the ones to start new conversations and like exploring different possibilities in how they live-they don't like sticking to the usual routine.

Introverted Intuition (Ni)

Mainly handles abstract thinking and insights, and helps with perceiving and understanding things that aren't easy to see or touch, like symbols, metaphors, formulas, and philosophical statements. People with high Ni are often very sensitive to mysterious or abstract matters; complex philosophy and clever metaphors can be deeply appreciated by them. They also tend to have strong premonitions and a sense of control over the overall development of things and the bigger picture, even if it's hard for them to explain why their intuition is so accurate. Those with high Ni often directly feel the limitations of language as a tool for expression, which is probably the best proof of introverted intuition.


Judgement

Perceiving functions correspond to different aspects of gathering information. After collecting information, we also need to make decisions, and that's when the judging function comes into play. The standard for making judgments can come from non-personal, objective values, which Jung called Thinking, or from personalized, subjective values, which Jung called Feeling. Both Thinking and Feeling are further divided into extraverted and introverted types, and everyone has all four functions, just in different strengths and levels of proficiency.

Extraverted Thinking (Te)

Mainly focuses on making things around oneself operate according to efficiency-first standards, optimizing external resources as much as possible, and maintaining control over external activities. People with strong extraverted thinking often have strong leadership skills, are good at allocating different resources to the most suitable places, and keep everything organized; when making decisions and interacting with others, they tend to be very straightforward. To improve efficiency and achieve objective goals, what they dislike most is beating around the bush. In their view, solving problems is more important than considering feelings or understanding principles.

Introverted Thinking (Ti)

mainly focuses on analyzing the internal logic and operational rules of things, emphasizing structured thinking, analyzing the principles and cause-and-effect relationships of things, while also predicting possible outcomes. People with high levels of introverted thinking have rigorous logical thinking and can analyze the causes and effects of things like solving a proof question, breaking things down step by step. They are good at spotting logical flaws, enjoy digging deep, and pay special attention to the consistency of logical chains and definitions. For example, if you say Xiaoming is a kind person, they might ask, how exactly do you define kindness? This is a typical trait of introverted thinkers.


Extraverted Feeling (Fe)

Its main role is to pick up on and resonate with the emotional impulses of others, keeping the surrounding environment harmonious and happy. People who use Fe are very sensitive to external emotions, good at putting themselves in others' shoes and empathizing, and can respond well to emotional needs. Their pursuit of the state of their surroundings often prioritizes 'harmony' over 'efficiency,' so their decisions tend to align with widely accepted moral standards. They emphasize taking care of everyone's feelings and generally dislike directly pointing out others' flaws or mistakes, preferring to express themselves subtly to avoid hurting anyone.

Introverted Feeling (Fi)

Its main role is dealing with deep internal emotional impulses and values, serving as a reflection of a person's core inner self. Its expressions are quite diverse because each person's inherent values differ — meaning, even if two people both have high Fi, one might hold Marxist beliefs while the other follows Islam. People with strong Fi are introspective. Rather than being influenced and changed by the outside world, they tend to listen to their inner voice. They’re willing to spend time and energy asking themselves about their desires and the values they aspire to, and they may insist that their emotions be directly expressed without outside constraint. Phrases like 'I have to act according to my true self' or 'Don’t interfere with my life' are often things Fi-dominant people say.
